## Jump right to:
- 5:43 Language Thing Of The Day: Transitivity
- 34:55 Question 1: Are accents predictable? That is, there are specific accents people have based on the languages they have learned, and often these have specific-enough features to have stereotypes. But would a native speaker of Parisian French have the stereotypical “French accent” when speaking English even if they had grown up in a cultural vacuum or learned English from a book? Further, if this is predictable like this, is it sufficient to predict the accent a native speaker of Quenya or Lojban might have when they were learning English the first time?
- 51:37 Question 2: Have you noticed people using [ts] instead of [t] at the beginning of words, and why might that happen?
- 1:04:34 Question 3: How do songs in tonal languages work? How do the speakers distinguish between the melody and the tone?
- 1:13:01 The puzzler: Change one letter each in the names of two rival NFL teams to get synonyms for the name of a third NFL team

## Covered in this episode:

## Links and other post-show thoughts:
- [Pseudo-reflexive verbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intransitive_verb#Ambitransitivity) in Romance languages (i.e. “i bathe myself” etc)? 
- In re hypothetical tri-transitive verbs: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_verb) suggests “bet” and “trade”, citing a [paper](https://benjamins.com/catalog/daslu.1.09mit) we couldn’t actually access, but you can try to dig it up if you want to read more. [Not everyone agrees, though](https://slate.com/human-interest/2014/04/does-english-have-any-tritransitive-verbs.html)
- “[Complex](https://www.thoughtco.com/what-is-complex-transitive-verbs-1689888) transitive [verb](https://www.polysyllabic.com/?q=navigating/verbphrase/complextrans)” can mean [different things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitive_verb#:~:text=Such%20constructions%20are%20sometimes%20called%20complex%20transitive.%20The%20category%20of%20complex%20transitives%20includes%20not%20only%20prepositional%20phrases%20but%20also%20dependent%20clauses%2C%20appositives%2C%20and%20other%20structures) and not everyone agrees on that, either
- [Unaccusative vs. unergative intransitive verbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intransitive_verb#Unaccusative_and_unergative_verbs) in English (depending on which argument is missing)
- [Valency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valency_(linguistics)), aka the real word for the marble slots we talked about
- Mary Spender’s [youtube channel](https://youtube.com/@MarySpender?si=HL33YtS45-fxbnpe)
- Chris Punsalan and his grandma of [Chooseday](https://youtube.com/shorts/UOfOPFMC3zw?si=0Y1MNAiDlhxRywLL) fame (Grandma has passed away since we recorded this episode, but there is an extensive backlog of her being very sweet if that's your thing)
- ⟨Triangle⟩ spelled as ⟨chriego⟩ because [kids are very good at phonetics actually](https://www.reddit.com/media?url=https%3A%2F%2Fi.redd.it%2Ftwoqaq50zf9b1.jpg)
- Okay, in retrospect, it should have been more obvious that [Chinese media using hardcoded subtitles more often than English media](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367603427_Research_on_the_Differences_in_the_Use_of_Subtitles_in_China_and_Abroad_from_the_Perspective_of_New_Media) relates as much or more to the [HUGE number of topolects](https://chinese.stackexchange.com/questions/14092/why-do-chinese-people-always-use-chinese-subtitles) in their media market than difficulty hearing tones (even in music) 
- [Old/Classical/Archaic Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Chinese) is [now suspected to be atonal](https://chinese.stackexchange.com/questions/7079/did-ancient-chinese-have-tones-%e5%a3%b0%e8%b0%83), but [Middle/Ancient Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Chinese) aka Qieyun *did* have tones and overlaps the written record of Chinese music, including the establishment of [Yayue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayue) and [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_China#Chinese_opera) which does appear to make use of tones. (This is extremely complex and if you’re interested, you should do a lot more digging yourself! Our post-production research is still limited ^^;)
